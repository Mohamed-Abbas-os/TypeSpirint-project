Lesson 07

Common typing mistakes are patterns, not verdicts.

A useful error is specific enough to practise. Replace “I am bad at typing” with a pattern you can observe.

Rushing familiar words

Familiarity can make attention skip ahead. Practise the troublesome word in isolation, then place it in a short sentence. Keep the pace just below the point where the error appears.

Looking down after a pause

Pauses can make the keyboard feel unfamiliar again. Use the F and J anchors, breathe out and restart the next word rather than searching across the whole row.

Over-correcting

One wrong character can become a cluster of backspaces if you panic. Pause, identify the caret position and make one calm correction. TypeSprint keeps backspace available so recovery is part of the skill.

Exercise: collect three patterns

  1. Run a short test or practice passage.
  2. Write down three exact errors, such as a missed comma or a repeated letter pair.
  3. Create a two-minute custom passage that includes those patterns.
  4. Retest later and compare the pattern, not only the score.

FAQ

Should I practise only my weakest keys?

Use focused drills, but return to normal sentences too. Transfer matters: a key that works in isolation should also feel natural in context.

Why do mistakes increase near the end?

Fatigue, rushing to finish or reduced attention can all play a role. Shorter sessions and a steady pace may reveal more than pushing through.
