Lesson 05

Touch typing for beginners.

You do not need to memorise the whole keyboard in one sitting. Build a small map, then expand it.

Stage one: feel the anchors

Start with the home row and the tactile bumps on F and J. Place your hands, type a few short patterns, then stop before fatigue makes the posture feel forced.

Stage two: add familiar words

Use easy words that repeat nearby letters. Practice mode lets you choose common words without a timer, so you can watch the passage instead of looking down after each key.

Stage three: introduce the eyes

Covering the keyboard completely can feel abrupt. Try placing a small reminder near the lower edge of the screen and practise looking at the passage for one short line at a time.

A beginner routine

  1. One minute of home-row patterns.
  2. Two minutes of common words.
  3. One sentence at a comfortable pace.
  4. One note about what felt easier or harder.

FAQ

What if I keep looking down?

Notice it without scolding yourself. Return your index fingers to the anchors, glance at the passage and try one shorter drill.

When should I take a speed test?

Whenever a baseline would help, but keep the result in context. Early practice is about learning the map, not proving a number.
