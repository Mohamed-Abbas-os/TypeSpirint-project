Lesson 04

Correct finger placement starts with a comfortable home.

The goal is not to force a rigid pose. It is to make common reaches predictable and keep the hands relaxed.

Find the home row

On a standard QWERTY keyboard, many learners begin with the left fingers near A, S, D and F and the right fingers near J, K, L and the semicolon key. The small bumps on F and J can help you return without looking.

Keep the movement economical

Let a finger reach and return instead of sliding the whole hand toward a key. Keep wrists neutral enough to feel comfortable and avoid pressing with more force than the keyboard requires.

Exercise: anchor and reach

  1. Place the index fingers on F and J.
  2. Type short patterns such as fa fj ja jf slowly.
  3. Add nearby keys: ded kik sas lol.
  4. Move into a sentence only when the return to home feels natural.

Adapt without guilt

Keyboard layouts, hand sizes and accessibility needs vary. A standard map is a starting point, not a test of correctness. Choose a setup that reduces strain and supports consistent movement.

FAQ

Do I need to use every finger?

No single map is mandatory. Learning a more distributed movement can reduce awkward reaches, but comfort and accessibility come first.

Why do F and J have bumps?

They provide a tactile reference for the index fingers, which can help you re-centre without looking down.
