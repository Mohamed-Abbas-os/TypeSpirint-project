Lesson 01

How to improve typing speed without losing control.

Faster typing is usually the result of fewer hesitations, not a permanent decision to press harder.

Start by protecting accuracy

If every sentence includes several corrections, your hands spend part of the session retracing their path. Begin at a pace where most keystrokes feel deliberate. TypeSprint's untimed practice is useful for finding that pace.

Use short, repeatable blocks

Try three rounds of two or three minutes: warm up with common words, practise one weak area, then finish with a comfortable sentence. Leave a short pause between rounds and write down one observation rather than chasing a new record every time.

Read slightly ahead

Let your eyes take in the next word while your fingers finish the current one. If looking ahead makes you tense, shorten the distance. The goal is preparation, not prediction.

Exercise: the clean-line loop

  1. Choose an intermediate sentence passage.
  2. Type it once at a calm pace.
  3. Repeat it with the same pace while trying to remove one hesitation.
  4. Finish with a timed test and compare accuracy as well as net WPM.
Speed that can be repeated is more useful than speed that can only be reached once.

FAQ

Should I practise speed or accuracy first?

Start with the highest pace at which accuracy and comfort remain stable. Speed can then grow around a reliable base.

How often should I practise?

Choose a schedule you can return to. A short session several times a week is easier to learn from than an occasional marathon.
