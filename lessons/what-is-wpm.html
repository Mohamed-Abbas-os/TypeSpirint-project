Lesson 02

What is WPM?

Words per minute is a convenient label for typing pace. The important part is knowing what a site counts as a word.

The five-character standard

TypeSprint treats every five typed characters, including spaces, as one standard word. This makes results comparable even when passages contain long or short real words.

Gross WPM

(total typed characters ÷ 5) ÷ elapsed minutes

Net WPM

gross WPM − (incorrect characters ÷ 5 ÷ elapsed minutes)

A small example

Imagine 250 characters typed in two minutes, with 10 incorrect characters. Gross WPM is `(250 ÷ 5) ÷ 2`, or 25. Net WPM subtracts the error rate: `(10 ÷ 5) ÷ 2`, which is 1, so the net result is 24 WPM.

Why net WPM helps

Gross pace shows movement. Net pace adds a cost for incorrect characters so a burst of fast but inaccurate typing does not look identical to clean typing at the same speed. It is still a practice metric, not a judgment of your ability.

Exercise: calculate before you compare

  1. Take a short TypeSprint test.
  2. Write down total characters, incorrect characters and seconds.
  3. Calculate gross and net WPM with the formulas above.
  4. Compare your result with the screen and investigate any difference.

FAQ

Why is a word five characters?

It is a convention that gives a consistent unit even when real words vary in length.

Is a higher WPM always better?

No. Context, accuracy, comfort and the ability to repeat the result all matter.
