Lesson 06

How to practise without looking at the keyboard.

Make the visual handoff gradual: the passage gets your attention, while your fingers learn the return path.

Reduce the problem

Do not begin with a long paragraph full of symbols. Start with short words and a familiar home row. In practice mode, choose a category and keep the passage short enough to repeat comfortably.

Use tactile anchors

Feel for the small bumps on F and J, or use the edge of the keyboard as a reference. These cues let you re-centre without searching visually for every key.

Try the screen-away drill

  1. Read a three- to five-word chunk.
  2. Look at the passage, not the keyboard, while typing it.
  3. Pause if you lose your place and return to the anchors.
  4. Repeat the same chunk once before moving on.

Keep it physically comfortable

Looking away should not mean reaching awkwardly or holding the wrists rigid. Adjust the keyboard, chair or text position so the exercise remains relaxed.

FAQ

Should I cover the keys?

Only if it helps. A gentle reminder or simply moving the eyes back to the passage can be enough.

What if my accuracy drops?

Shorten the chunk, slow down and return to untimed practice. Accuracy is information about the drill, not a reason to push through tension.
